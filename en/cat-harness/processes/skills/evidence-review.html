Evidence review — the same rule, a second instance

Skill evidence-review: Evidence review as an instance of untainted verification — the separation of the appraising party from the drafting party, what recurring surveillance in a living guideline shares with a stale QA verdict, and which of guideline methodology’s rules are fenced rather than shipped.

Named by: Is the incremental IG AST what a full build would have produced?, Incremental IG build, L3 FHIR IG pipeline, Contributor feedback on a working draft, Make a decided public comment change

Skill id: evidence-review · Package: folio-core

untainted-verification states a generic discipline. Evidence review — the appraisal of the evidence behind a recommendation — is one instance of it; the translation round trip is another. Two unrelated instances are evidence that the discipline is generic, where one would only be an example.

The rule

The party that drafted the recommendation does not write the evidence appraisal for it.

Guideline methodology already separates the systematic review team from the guideline development group for the same reason: a party assessing its own output is comparing a thing with its own paraphrase of itself.

untainted-verification here
producer whoever drafts the recommendation
checker — given the artefact, and nothing that would let it shortcut the appraisal, given the question and the evidence, not the draft’s conclusion
adjudicator — given the intent and the checker’s output, never the artefact the evidence-to-decision judgement

An appraiser shown the drafted recommendation is told the answer before being asked the question; its “the evidence supports it” measures the instruction, not the literature.

Recurring surveillance is staleness, generalised

A living guideline is updated as evidence appears, so its appraisal is a verdict with an expiry. A QA verdict is hashed to its subject and stales when the subject is edited; a living-guideline verdict stales when the evidence base changes, even though the subject did not.

The field_hash covers only inputs in the repository; an external corpus is not covered. Either way, “verified on DATE against THESE inputs” is a different claim from “verified”, because a reader given the second cannot tell a current verdict from a lapsed one. A folio implementing surveillance gives each verdict an explicit expiry, as bean-blocking (opt-in cat-harness.work-plan) does for a block: a verdict with no expiry cannot be told from an abandoned one.

The fencing analysis — which rules are the platform’s, and which are not

Run through domain-fencing (opt-in cat-harness.kg-ops)’s three questions, in order, first-to-answer wins:

candidate rule Q1 rationale survives translation? Q2 domain in mechanism or vocabulary? Q3 needs the domain installed? verdict
appraiser ≠ drafter yes, word for word vocabulary no platform — it IS the spine
a verdict carries what it was verified against, and when yes — it is the field_hash argument vocabulary no platform
a finding is upheld by opening the cited source, never by trusting the rule’s wording yes vocabulary no platform — and already the practice in voice-who-guideline-development
certainty rated across the GRADE domains no mechanism yes fence
PICO question formulation no mechanism yes fence
evidence-to-decision framework tables no mechanism yes fence
a search strategy is re-runnable — databases, dates, query recorded yes in rationale mechanism is bibliographic yes fence the mechanism, keep the rationale (it is the row above)

Three rows are platform rules; the four fenced rows are a folio’s methodology, not a backlog. Nothing is shipped for the fenced rows — empty criteria would let a consumer scan nothing and report a clean run. A folio that wants them declares them in its own repository.

What this skill does NOT do

It asserts no clinical or empirical claim, and the platform must not. The mechanism carries process — who may rule on what, given what. The evidence is the folio’s and the judgement is the guideline group’s; a platform that shipped a default answer to either would be asserting on their behalf.
